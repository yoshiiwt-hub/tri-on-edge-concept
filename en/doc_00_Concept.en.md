## 1. Overview (What is Tri-On-Edge?)

　Tri-On-Edge is a concept for manufacturing-site data processing that connects a site's equipment control systems (OT) with servers, the cloud, and AI systems (IT) at the edge (the intermediate zone, IoT), capturing real-time changes in data generated at the manufacturing site and converting them into value.

　Tri-On-Edge is not merely a data collection and storage system; it aims to embody the "spirit of KAIZEN" that flows through the manufacturing site using digital technology (OT, IT, IoT), so that people and systems can grow together in collaboration.

　This concept was inspired by hands-on experience in software development and operation at the OT-IT boundary (the edge), and represents an independent conception by the author personally, containing no specific implementation or intellectual property belonging to any particular organization (source code, configuration data, internal documents, etc.).

## 2. Core Concept (What is the underlying idea?)

　Tri-On-Edge is built on the idea of the **"Three Tri's" ("Tri-Tri's")**.

### 1. Trigger (Function: capturing what is happening on-site)

　A Trigger is the mechanism that detects a meaningful point of change within a continuous stream of data and initiates an action.

- Handles cyclic waveform (time-series, stream) data generated by manufacturing-site operations (processing, transport, inspection, etc.)
- Focuses not on mere recording or storage, but on "detecting change"
- Performs active detection and response, not just passive log-keeping

　Tri-On-Edge emphasizes not just collecting data, but "capturing what happened at the manufacturing site."

### 2. Trial (Spirit: verification and challenge)

　Trial refers to the attitude of actually running, trying, and confirming things on-site — rather than debating theory in a conference room.

　It also represents a spirit of embracing failure as a learning opportunity, and continuously improving through repeated practice.

- A lightweight, modular structure for cycling through Try and Error quickly
- Assumes continuous operation on the manufacturing floor
- Not something completed once and left alone, but continuously evolved

　Tri-On-Edge is not a mechanism that provides a fixed correct answer, but "a mechanism for continually approaching the correct answer." It values the mindset of Lean, Agile, and KAIZEN.

### 3. Trinity (Structure: unity of three, collaboration and coordination)

　Tri-On-Edge assumes that stakeholders collaborate and coordinate with one another.

- Factory Guys (Domain Experts) — hold domain knowledge of the manufacturing site, products, and equipment, and sense issues and anomalies
- System Engineers (IT Experts) — build the system and ensure stable operation
- Data Scientists (Analysis / AI Experts) — extract meaning from data and convert it into value

　This three-way unity aims to connect the following three domains at the edge, rather than letting them remain siloed:

- OT (manufacturing site / equipment control)
- IT (cloud / servers)
- Data (analysis / AI)

## 3. System Domains (What areas does it cover?)

　Tri-On-Edge covers the following four system domains (the "4S"). Every Tri-On-Edge function belongs to exactly one of these domains.

### 1. Supervise (monitoring, management, operations)

　Supervise is the domain of Observability that allows the system to "keep running, stay current, and operate over long durations without stopping."

- Role: "protect the discipline and health of the system, and enable continuous use"
- Target data: process state, resources such as disk/memory, system configuration
- Actions:
  - Watch and help: liveness monitoring, automatic recovery, prevention of resource exhaustion
  - Store and distribute: storage and distribution of configuration and code via GitOps, etc.

### 2. Stream (real-time data)

　Stream is the "moving world," premised on the unknown, the undetermined, and fluctuation.

- Role: capture the manufacturing site's present moment and respond immediately
- Target data: continuously flowing, not-yet-finalized data
- Actions:
  - Find (detect / collect): real-time monitoring, waveform cut-out
  - Act (act / save / connect): feedback to equipment, notification to the manufacturing site, data connection to IT/cloud

### 3. Stored (accumulated data)

　Stored is the "world to look back on," emphasizing reproducibility and verifiability.

- Role: learn from the past and apply it to the future
- Target data: finalized, reproducible accumulated data
- Actions:
  - Analyze: extract trends and latent insights
  - Learn: build AI models, validate accuracy, and tune

### 4. Simulate (virtual verification)

　Simulate is the world where data can be used to perform virtual verification (virtual commissioning) ahead of the physical world.

- Role: try out the "future" and the "unknown," and search for the optimal solution (hypothesis)
- Target data: virtual verification simulation models
- Actions:
  - Replay tests using past data
  - Simulation of new parameters
  - Automated testing (CI)

## 4. Principles of Action (What principles guide behavior?)

　The following principles guide Tri-On-Edge's product development and project execution.

### 1. Understand and respect the manufacturing-site / TPS mindset, and apply it to software development and operations

- **Genchi Genbutsu (現地・現物 — go and see the actual place and the actual thing)**

  - Prioritize the real physical phenomena (facts) at the manufacturing site and the real data that represents them.
  - Prioritize implementation and verification, not just design and assumption.

- **Naze Naze (なぜなぜ — ask "why" repeatedly)**

  - Pursue not only "what," but "why" — the underlying principles and reasons.

- **Jidoka (自働化 — automation with a human touch)**, **"don't make people watchdogs for machines"**

  - The system detects its own abnormalities and notifies people.

- **Just-In-Time**, **"eliminate Muri, Mura, and Muda (overburden, unevenness, and waste)"**, **the Seven Wastes (processing, inventory, defects/rework, waiting, overproduction, motion, transport)**

  - Perform only the necessary processing, only when it's needed. Avoid unnecessary resource consumption and variation.

- **4S (Seiri, Seiton, Seisou, Seiketsu — sort, set in order, shine, standardize)**, **the 5 Fixed Points (fixed route, fixed quantity, fixed location, fixed name, fixed color)**

  - Maintain a managed state so that resources can be used smoothly and effectively, free of Muri, Mura, and Muda.

- **The Seven Wastes of Knowledge Work (meetings, backroom dealing, materials, coordination, managers' pride, complacency, and "playing at" work)**

  - Apply these to project execution and decision-making.

### 2. Improve QCD by combining digital solutions with analog means, rather than relying on digital alone

- Compare software-based automation against analog means involving people and physical objects, from a QCD perspective that considers not just development but also long-term assurance and operation.
- Examples of analog means worth considering: manual work performed by people (standardized to reduce dependence on specific individuals), manual checks and confirmation by people (with judgment criteria made explicit), the use of posted notices and signboards (Genchi Genbutsu), and so on.

### 3. Will 1st, Rule 2nd, Tool Later

- Don't start from the means. Think in the order of Will (what you want to do, the ideal state, the state you should be in) → Rule (documented rules, standards) → Tool (means, technology, execution method).
- That said, drawing inspiration for the ideal state from what a given means or technology can achieve is encouraged.

## 5. Keywords (What are the important terms?)

### Trigger (execution unit)

　The execution unit of processing within the software (generally called a "Worker" in the IT field) is called a Trigger.

### Segment (cyclic waveform data)

　Time-series, waveform, or stream data that recurs cyclically through manufacturing-site activity (processing, inspection, etc.) is called a Trigger Segment (Segment).

　A Segment spans one interval, from the start of one Trigger action (Trigger On) to its completion (Trigger Off).

## 6. Summary

Tri-On-Edge is:

1. capturing the site's present moment through Trigger,
2. verifying it on-site through Trial, and
3. connecting people and technology through Trinity —

　a body of thought and implementation for manufacturing-site data processing software, built to take on a VUCA (complex, uncertain) era.

　This concept aims to give a consistent worldview across code, operations, and education, so that everyone involved can discuss things using shared thinking and shared language.

### Project Name

　The ongoing effort to build, operate, and continuously improve Tri-On-Edge is called the **Tri-On Project**, a name that carries the intent to "keep trying on" (Try On).

　The essence of the Tri-On Project is the mindset of its members themselves, and the invisible energy that flows from it. For this reason, the Tri-On Project does not exclude other projects — it proceeds as part of other projects, or in coordination and collaboration with them.

### Slogan

　"Try on the Edge Now, KAIZEN the Future."

　(Try it on-site now, and KAIZEN the future.)
